Separable Phrasal Verbs

bring back · call up · cross out · do over · drop off

Level 3 — Grammar Review

 

What Is a Separable Phrasal Verb?

A phrasal verb is a verb + a small word (a particle): call + up, drop + off. Together they make one new meaning.

Separable means the object can go between the two parts.
verb + object + particle  or  verb + particle + noun
✔ Please call the dentist up.    ✔ Please call up the dentist.

Today's five verbs are all separable.

 

The Five Verbs

Phrasal VerbMeaningExample
bring backreturn something to its place or ownerBring back my pen when you finish.
call upphone a person or a placeWe called up the hotel.
cross output a line through a wordCross out the extra word.
do overdo a job again from the startThe chef had to do the soup over.
drop offtake a person or thing to a place and leave it thereI'll drop you off at the station.
 

The One Rule: Nouns and Pronouns

Noun object → it can go in the middle OR at the end.
✔ drop off the mail    ✔ drop the mail off
Pronoun object (me, you, him, her, it, us, them) → it MUST go in the middle.
✔ drop it off    ✘ drop off it
Quick check: Do you see a pronoun right after up, out, back, over, or off? Move it to the middle.

Exercise 2 — Change the Noun to a Pronoun

Say the sentence again. Change the CAPITALIZED words to a pronoun. Then click to check.

1. Can you drop off THE TWINS at the pool?

Can you drop them off at the pool? — the twins → them, in the middle.

2. Please cross MY PHONE NUMBER out.

Please cross it out. — my phone number → it.

3. Mom called up MR. SILVA this morning.

Mom called him up this morning. — Mr. Silva → him.

4. The workers have to do over THE TILE FLOOR.

The workers have to do it over. — the tile floor → it.

5. Did you bring back MY SCISSORS?

Did you bring them back? — scissors are plural → them.

6. The driver dropped off MY WIFE AND ME near the park.

The driver dropped us off near the park. — my wife and me → us.

 

Tricky Pair: bring back or drop off?

VerbThink…Example
bring backIt goes back to where it came from, or to its owner.Please bring back the ruler you took from my desk.
drop offYou take it to a new place and leave it there.I dropped off the rent at the office.
Quick check: drop off often has at + a place. bring back often has to + the owner or the first place.

Exercise 4 — Mixed Practice

Choose the verb, use the correct form, and put it around the pronoun. Then click to check.

1. Grandpa couldn't drive to the clinic this morning, so my sister _____ him _____ there.

my sister dropped him off — took him to a place and left him there. Past tense: dropped.

2. Your new neighbors seem nice. Why don't you _____ them _____ and invite them for coffee?

Why don't you call them up — phone them. Base form after "why don't you".

3. The magician's first trick failed last night, so he _____ it _____.

he did it over — did it again. Past tense: did.

4. The price on this tag is old. Please _____ it _____ and write the new price.

Please cross it out — put a line through it.

5. I took this chair from the room next door. After the meeting, I'll _____ it _____ there.

I'll bring it back — return it to its first place.
