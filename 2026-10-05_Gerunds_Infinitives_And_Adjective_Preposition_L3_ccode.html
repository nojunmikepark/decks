Gerunds, Infinitives, and Adjective + Preposition

verb + -ing, to + verb, and adjective + preposition + -ing

Level 3 — Grammar Review

 

Two Verb Forms After a Verb

Some verbs need a second verb after them. The second verb has one of two forms.

Gerund = verb + -ing  →  walking, eating, writing
Infinitive = to + base verb  →  to walk, to eat, to write
Lily enjoys baking cookies with her nephew.
Lily wants to bake a cake for the office.

The FIRST verb decides the form. Learn each verb with its form.

 

Verb + Gerund (-ing)

Rule: After these verbs, use verb + -ing.
advise · avoid · consider · deny · dislike · enjoy · finish · postpone · practice
Tom finished mowing the lawn at noon.
My aunt dislikes driving in the snow.
We postponed opening the gift until Grandpa arrived.
Watch out: She enjoys to bake. → She enjoys baking.
 

Verb + Infinitive (to + verb)

Rule: After these verbs, use to + base verb.
agree · ask · decide · expect · learn · need · offer · promise · want · wish · would like
The clerk agreed to change my seat.
I would like to sit by the window, please.
Our boss decided to close early on Friday.
Watch out: He decided going home. → He decided to go home.
 

Verbs That Take Both Forms

Rule: These verbs can take -ing OR to + verb. The meaning stays the same.
begin · continue · hate · like · love · start · plan · can't stand
It began snowing at midnight. = It began to snow at midnight.
Ivan loves fishing. = Ivan loves to fish.

Careful: would like is NOT in this group. It takes only to + verb: I would like to rest.

Adjective + Preposition + -ing

Rule: Many adjectives go with a set preposition. After the preposition, use verb + -ing. Never use to + verb after a preposition.
PrepositionAdjectives
ofafraid of · tired of · proud of · fond of · sick of · scared of
aboutexcited about · worried about · nervous about · upset about · curious about
atgood at · bad at
ininterested in
forfamous for · responsible for · thankful for
withbored with
Tina is excited about visiting her new grandson.
Marta is good at solving puzzles.
Watch out: He is good at solve puzzles. → He is good at solving puzzles.
 

Exercise 2 — Preposition + -ing

Write the preposition and the -ing form of the verb. Then click to check.

1. I am really bad ____ (remember) people's names.

at remembering (or about remembering) — bad at + -ing.

2. We were thankful ____ (get) a ride home in the storm.

for getting — thankful for + -ing.

3. Lucy got sick ____ (wait) for the slow elevator, so she took the stairs.

of waiting — sick of + -ing.

4. My grandpa has always been fond ____ (tell) jokes.

of telling — fond of + -ing.

5. The players were upset ____ (lose) the final game.

about losing (or at losing / over losing) — upset about + -ing.

6. Are you curious ____ (try) the new Thai restaurant?

about trying — curious about + -ing.

7. Which students are interested ____ (join) the chess club?

in joining — interested in + -ing.

8. Before the interview, I was worried ____ (say) the wrong thing.

about saying — worried about + -ing.

 

Exercise 3 — Fix the Mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Correct the CAPITALIZED word or words. Then click to check.

1. My cousin wants BUYING a motorcycle.

to buy — Want takes to + verb: My cousin wants to buy a motorcycle.

2. Mrs. Hall is afraid of FLY, so she takes the train.

flying — After the preposition of, use -ing.

3. We postponed TO MOVE to a new apartment until spring.

moving — Postpone takes verb + -ing.

4. Nadia promised CALLING her mother every Sunday.

to call — Promise takes to + verb.

5. Jun is excited about START his new job on Monday.

starting — After the preposition about, use -ing.

6. Did you finish TO READ that book?

reading — Finish takes verb + -ing.
