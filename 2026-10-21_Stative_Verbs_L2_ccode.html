Stative Verbs

Is it a state or an action?

Level 2 — Grammar

 

What Is a Stative Verb?

Action verbsStative verbs
Meaningsomething you doa feeling, a thought, owning, or a sense
Examplesjump, bake, clean, readlove, doubt, own, see
SentenceSam is baking bread right now.Sam loves fresh bread.

Ask: "Is it something I DO?" Yes = action. No = state.

 

Groups of Stative Verbs

Feelings: like, love, hate, want, need, prefer
Thinking: know, believe, understand, remember, mean
Owning: have, own, belong
Senses: see, hear, smell, taste
Describing: seem, look (= seem), sound
These cookies look delicious.
Your idea sounds fun.
 

The Big Rule

Stative verbs use the simple present, even right now. No -ing.
Action verbs use the simple present for habits and the present continuous for now.
Right now, the kids are building a snowman. (action)
Right now, my students want a break. (state)
Remember: he / she / it + verb + -s.   Questions: Do / Does.   Negatives: don't / doesn't.

Verbs With Two Meanings

Some verbs can be a state OR an action. The meaning decides.

VerbState (no -ing)Action (-ing is OK)
haveowning: Paulo has a big family.eating: Paulo is having breakfast.
thinkopinion: I think this bag is too expensive.using your mind: I am thinking about my vacation.
tastehow food is: This cake tastes sweet.trying food: Grandma is tasting the cake batter.
smellhow something is: These flowers smell nice.using your nose: The chef is smelling the fish.

Common Mistakes

✗ Kim is preferring tea.   ✓ Kim prefers tea.
✗ My son want a new game.   ✓ My son wants a new game.
✗ Are you remember me?   ✓ Do you remember me?
✗ Look! It rains.   ✓ Look! It is raining. (action)
 

Exercise 3 — Find and Fix the Mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Say the correct sentence, then click to check.

1. We are knowing our neighbors well.

We know our neighbors well. — "Know" is a state. No -ing.

2. This shirt is costing too much.

This shirt costs too much. — "Cost" is a state. This shirt = it: costs.

3. Look! The cat sleep on my bed.

Look! The cat is sleeping on my bed. — "Sleep" is an action, and "Look!" = now.

4. My mother speak three languages.

My mother speaks three languages. — My mother = she: add -s.

5. Is she like coffee?

Does she like coffee? — Questions with stative verbs use Do / Does.

6. They are need more time.

They need more time. — No am / is / are before a simple present verb.
