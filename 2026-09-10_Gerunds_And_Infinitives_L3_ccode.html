Gerunds and Infinitives

Level 3 — Grammar Practice

-ing form  •  to + verb  •  either one

 

Two Forms After a Verb

When one verb follows another verb, the second verb changes form.

Gerund: verb + -ing  →  She keeps a diary. She enjoys writing.
Infinitive: to + base verb  →  He needs a job. He wants to work.

The first verb tells you which form to use. There is no rule you can hear — you learn the lists.

 

Group 1 — Verb + -ing

advise, avoid, consider, deny, dislike, enjoy, finish, postpone, practice

The judge advised paying the ticket online.

My brother avoids flying in small planes.

We considered hiring a plumber.

She finished packing at midnight.

Quick check: After these verbs, never write to.

Group 2 — Verb + to + verb

agree, ask, decide, expect, learn, need, offer, plan, promise, want, would like

The bank agreed to lower the fee.

My neighbor learned to fix his own bicycle.

We expect to hear from the school on Monday.

Would you like to try the soup?

Quick check: After these verbs, always write to + the base verb — never to + -ing.

Group 3 — Either Form

begin, can’t stand, continue, hate, like, love, start
Both forms are correct, and the meaning is the same.
Verb-ing formto + verb
beginThe baby began crying.The baby began to cry.
hateI hate cleaning the oven.I hate to clean the oven.
continueHe continued reading.He continued to read.
 

Practice 3 — Either Form

Preposition + -ing

After any preposition (of, about, at, in, for, on), use the -ing form.

She is good at remembering names.

They are tired of paying for parking.

We are thinking about painting the hallway.

Thank you for coming so early.

Watch out: to is sometimes a preposition — look forward to seeing you.
