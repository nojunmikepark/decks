Grammar · Level 2 · Modals Form 2

Be Able To

Only "be" changes

be + able to + base verb
 

Part 1 · Now

am / is / are able to

SubjectBeExample
IamI am able to whistle.
he / she / itisOmar is able to sew.
you / we / theyareThe twins are able to skate.
subject + am / is / are + able to + base verb
 

Part 2 · Past

was / were able to

SubjectBeExample
I / he / she / itwasKim was able to win the race.
you / we / theywereWe were able to park near the beach.
Watch out: "able to" never changes. Only be changes: am, is, are, was, were.
 

Part 3 · No and questions

Negative and question

Negative: Omar isn't able to knit.   We weren't able to sleep.
Question: Is Omar able to knit?   Were they able to sleep?
Negative: be + not + able to + verb
Question: Be + subject + able to + verb?
Watch out: is able to sew (NOT is able sew). Never "doesn't able" and never "can to".

Practice 2 · 1–2

Find and Fix

Each sentence has one mistake in CAPITAL letters. Say the correct sentence, then click to check.

1. We WAS able to catch the early train.

We were able to catch the early train. (or: We are able to catch the early train.) — We + were (past) or are (now). Never was.

2. Is he able to SPEAKS Korean?

Is he able to speak Korean? — After to, use the base verb. Only be changes.

 

Practice 2 · 3–4

Find and Fix

Each sentence has one mistake in CAPITAL letters. Say the correct sentence, then click to check.

3. My neighbor IS ABLE PLAY the guitar.

My neighbor is able to play the guitar. (or: My neighbor was able to play / can play the guitar.) — Always keep "to": is able to play.

4. Last summer, I AM able to visit Peru.

Last summer, I was able to visit Peru. — "Last summer" = past: I was.

 

Practice 2 · 5–6

Find and Fix

Each sentence has one mistake in CAPITAL letters. Say the correct sentence, then click to check.

5. They aren't able to SWIMMING in the lake.

They aren't able to swim in the lake. — After to, use the base verb: swim.

6. IS your cousins able to stay for dinner?

Are your cousins able to stay for dinner? (or: Were your cousins able to stay for dinner?) — Your cousins = they: Are ...? (now) or Were ...? (past)
