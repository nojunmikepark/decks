Count vs Noncount Nouns Review

Can you count it?

Level 2 — Grammar Review

 

Core Idea — Can You Count It?

Count nouns are things you can count one by one. They have a plural form.

a ticket → three tickets  |  a coin → many coins

Noncount nouns are things you cannot count one by one: liquids, materials, ideas, and groups of small things. They have no plural form.

snow (not a snow, not snows)  |  luck  |  wood
Quick test: Can you say one ____, two ____s? Yes → count. No → noncount.

Noncount nouns always take a singular verb: The snow is deep.

 

Reference — What Goes With Each Type

Quick check: Can you make it plural? one coin → two coins, one child → two children = count. No plural = noncount.

Exercise 2 — is or are?

Say the answer first, then click to check.

1. The weather ____ nice today.

is — weather is noncount, so the verb is singular.

2. My keys ____ on the desk.

are — keys is plural, so the verb is plural.

3. Spanish ____ easy for me.

is — a language is a noncount noun.

4. The children ____ hungry.

are — children is an irregular plural.

5. The medicine ____ in the cabinet.

is — medicine is noncount.

6. Two men ____ fixing the roof.

are — men is an irregular plural.

 

How Much? How Many?

Count: many / a few + plural noun → many coins, a few tickets
Noncount: much / a little + noun → much snow, a little luck

Measure words let you count a noncount noun:

a / an / a number + measure word + of + noncount noun
a carton of eggs is count, but a carton of milk counts a noncount noun.
a drop of rain → two drops of rain  |  a pinch of pepper

The number changes the measure word, not the noncount noun: two drops of rain, never two drops of rains.

Exercise 5 — Find the Error

The word in CAPITAL LETTERS is wrong. Say the correct word, then click to check.

1. We saw A beautiful weather in Hawaii.

(no word) — We saw beautiful weather in Hawaii. Weather is noncount: no a.

2. Her jewelry ARE very expensive.

is — Her jewelry is very expensive. Jewelry is noncount: singular verb.

3. He asked me for A money.

some (or no word) — He asked me for some money. Money is noncount: not a.

4. The street has too MANY garbage.

much — The street has too much garbage. Garbage is noncount.

5. He wears nice CLOTHINGS.

clothing (or clothes) — He wears nice clothing. Clothing is noncount: no -s.

6. How MANY pollution is in the city?

much — How much pollution is in the city? Pollution is noncount.
