Time Markers: Simple Past, Present Perfect, Present Perfect Continuous

The little words that tell you which tense to use

Level 3 — Grammar

 

Time markers are clues

A time marker is a word or phrase that tells you when. Read the marker first, and it points to the tense.

Nora translated a contract LAST WEEKEND. → finished time → simple past
Nora has translated six contracts SO FAR this month. → counting up to now → present perfect
Nora has been translating the same contract SINCE BREAKFAST, and she is still not done. → still going on → present perfect continuous
What the marker tells you:
FINISHED (yesterday, ago, in 2008, at noon) → simple past
UP TO NOW (so far, ever, twice, since / for + a stative verb) → present perfect
STILL HAPPENING (all day, for / since + still, How long…?) → present perfect continuous
 

Simple past markers — the time is over

Finished time: yesterday · last night / week / month · two hours ago · in 2008 · at noon · when I was a child · the other day · until 2019
The ferry left at noon.
In 1972 my grandmother came to Texas.
Two months ago the gas station on the corner closed.
When I was in high school, I played the clarinet.
Watch out: ago always goes with the simple past. ✓ He called an hour ago. ✗ He has called an hour ago.
 

Exercise 1 — Pick the simple past marker

The verb is in the simple past. Say which marker in parentheses fits, then click to check.

1. The fireworks ended ____ (an hour ago / since an hour).

an hour ago — ago + a length of time = finished

2. ____ (Last Tuesday / So far), the vet gave our rabbit a shot.

Last Tuesday — last + a day = finished time

3. Reza’s uncle started a trucking company ____ (in 2004 / for 2004).

in 2004 — in + a year = finished time

4. ____ (When I was seven / Up to now), I got lost at the county fair.

When I was seven — a finished period of my life

5. The scholarship office called ____ (yesterday afternoon / lately) with good news.

yesterday afternoon — yesterday = finished time

6. We saw a whale from the pier ____ (the other day / so far).

the other day — the other day = a recent finished day

 

Present perfect markers — up to now

Up to now: so far · up to now · already · yet · just · ever / never · still (not) · lately / recently · twice / three times · this week (unfinished)
With stative verbs: for / since + know, have, be, own, want, like
Dev has owned a fishing boat for six years.
We have already ordered dessert.
Have you ever slept in a tent?
Ms. Abreu has given two quizzes so far this month.
I have not seen the new movie yet.

already and just sit between have and the participle. yet goes at the end of a negative or a question.

Present perfect continuous markers — still going

Still going on: for / since + still or right now · all day / all evening / all week · How long…? · lately / recently (with an action verb)
The kids have been jumping on the bed all evening, and the frame is creaking.
My neighbor has been practicing the tuba since noon.
How long has your daughter been studying Korean?
My back has been hurting lately.
Same markers, two tenses: for, since, lately work with both perfect tenses. Ask: is it still happening? → continuous. Is the verb stative (know, have, be)? → present perfect, never -ing.
 

Exercise 3 — Present perfect or continuous?

The CAPITALIZED marker is the clue. Say the verb form, then click to check.

1. The rooster next door ____ (crow) SINCE sunrise, and it is still going.

has been crowing — still going → present perfect continuous

2. Lily ____ (know) how to juggle SINCE she was ten.

has known — know is stative → present perfect

3. SO FAR the substitute teacher ____ (forget) only one student’s name.

has forgotten — so far + a count → present perfect

4. HOW LONG ____ the bread ____ (rise)?

has … been rising — How long + an action still in progress → present perfect continuous

5. The escalator at the mall ____ (be) out of order FOR two weeks.

has been — be is stative → present perfect, no -ing

6. The candles ____ (burn) ALL EVENING, and the room is still glowing.

have been burning — all evening + still → present perfect continuous

 

for · since · ago

MarkerWhat follows itTenseExample
fora length of time: for ten minutes, for a long timepresent perfect / continuousAli has been asleep for an hour.
sincea starting point: since Monday, since I was a childpresent perfect / continuousAli has been asleep since nine o’clock.
agoa length of time, counting back from now: an hour agosimple past onlyAli fell asleep an hour ago.
Quick test: Can you answer “How long?” → for. Can you answer “Starting when?” → since. Does the sentence look back from now to a finished moment? → ago.

Read the story one line at a time. The CAPITALIZED marker tells you the tense. Say the verb form, then click to check.
