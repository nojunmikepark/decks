Grammar · Level 2 · Modals 2

Necessary or Not?

must · have to · have got to · don't have to

modal + base verb
 

Part 1 · Necessary

must

Use must for something necessary, like a rule or a law.

Bakers must cover their hair.
All dogs in this city must get a license tag.
Watch out: must never changes, and we do not use to after it.
 

Part 2 · Necessary (rules and daily life)

have to / has to

have to means something is necessary. We use it for rules and for daily life.

SubjectFormExample
I / you / we / theyhave toWe have to vote.
he / she / ithas toJoy has to stay.
Question: Do / Does + subject + have to + base verb?   Does Joy have to stay?
 

Part 3 · Necessary (speaking)

have got to

People often say have got to in conversation. It means something is necessary.

SubjectFormExample
I / you / we / theyhave got toI've got to run!
he / she / ithas got toHe's got to rest.
 

Part 4 · Not necessary

don't have to / doesn't have to

don't have to means it is not necessary. You can do it or not. It is your choice.

SubjectFormExample
I / you / we / theydon't have toYou don't have to dress up.
he / she / itdoesn't have toMax doesn't have to shout.
 

Compare

Necessary or not necessary?

Necessary

Campers have to leave by noon. It is a rule.

Not necessary

Campers don't have to bring a tent. We have tents.
