Modals of Advice: should, ought to, had better

giving advice and warnings

Level 3 — Grammar Review

 

The Form

We use these modals to tell someone what is a good idea.

should + base verb  →  should go
ought to + base verb  →  ought to go
had better (’d better) + base verb  →  had better go
NegativeExample
should not / shouldn’tYou shouldn’t go.
had better notYou’d better not go.
ought not to (rare)You ought not to go.

No -s: she shoulds. No to after should or had better: should to go, had better to go.

 

should / shouldn’t = Advice

should = a good idea. shouldn’t = not a good idea. It is mild and friendly.
You should eat more vegetables.
Kids shouldn’t look at screens right before bed.
Watch out: Dad shoulds rest. → Dad should rest.
 

ought to = the Same as should

ought to has the same meaning as should. It sounds a little more formal. Keep to after ought.
Parents ought to read to their children every day.
= Parents should read to their children every day.
Watch out: You ought relax. → You ought to relax.
 

had better = a Warning

had better is stronger. It is a warning: if you don’t do it, something bad will happen. We often add or + the bad result.
We’d better unplug the space heater, or it will overheat.
You’d better not leave candles burning at night.
Watch out: had better to → had better · had not better → had better not
 

How Strong Is It?

ModalStrengthExample
should / ought tomild: a good ideaYou should visit the new bakery downtown.
had betterstrong: a warningYou’d better close the gate, or the dog will run out.

For a warning, must is also correct: You must close the gate. For friendly advice, use should or ought to.

Summary — Main Takeaways

ModalMeaningExample
should / shouldn’tadvice: a good idea (mild)You should wear comfortable shoes on a long walk.
ought tosame as should, more formalDrivers ought to keep a flashlight in the car.
had better / had better nota warning: or something bad will happenYou’d better not forget your keys, or you’ll be locked out.

Form: modal + base verb. No -s. Only ought keeps to.
