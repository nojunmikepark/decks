Present Perfect Continuous Without For and Since

Five ways to use it with no for or since

Level 3 — Grammar Practice

 

Core Idea

Form: have / has + been + verb-ing
Negative: haven't / hasn't been + verb-ing
Question: Have / Has + subject + been + verb-ing?

You already know this tense with for and since. But many sentences have no for or since. Then we look for other clues.

UseClue
1. Evidence nowYou can see, hear, smell, or feel the result now.
2. Excuse or reasonYou explain why something is true right now.
3. ComplaintSomeone keeps doing something that bothers you.
4. Repeated actionAgain and again up to now: all day, all week. No number.
5. Temporary situationTrue now, but not forever: lately, these days, this month.
 

Uses 1 and 2 — Evidence and Excuses

Evidence now: You see a result now, so you talk about the activity that made it.
Excuse or reason: You say sorry or explain with the activity.
The floor is wet and shiny. Someone has been mopping.
Your shoes are muddy. Have you been walking in the park?
Sorry, I can't talk long. I've been cooking dinner, and the pot is on the stove.
Excuse my messy hair. I've been working in the garden.
Watch out: The activity may be finished or still going. The proof is here now.
 

Exercise 1 — Evidence and Excuses

Write the present perfect continuous form of the verb. Then click to check.

1. Your hands are covered in blue paint. ________ (you / paint) the fence?

Have you been painting — The blue paint is the evidence now.

2. The streets are wet, and there are puddles everywhere. It ________ (rain).

has been raining — We see the wet streets now.

3. I'm sorry I forgot your birthday. I ________ (work) so hard this month.

have been working — An excuse: the reason for the problem.

4. There's flour all over the counter. What ________ (Grandma / make)?

has Grandma been making — Question form: has + subject + been + verb-ing.

5. Please excuse my late reply. I ________ (study) for my final exams.

have been studying — An excuse for the late reply.

6. Mario is out of breath, and his face is red. He ________ (run) up the stairs.

has been running — His red face is the evidence. He = has.

 

Uses 3 and 4 — Complaints and Repeated Actions

Complaint: Someone keeps doing something that bothers you. Often with again or an exclamation mark (!).
Repeated action: Something happens again and again up to now. Time clues: all day all week all morning.
My little brother has been using my headphones again!
The neighbors have been letting their dog bark at night.
The car alarm downstairs has been beeping all week.
My coworker has been begging me for rides all month.
No number! has been begging = again and again. A number (has begged four times) = present perfect.

Use 5 — Temporary Situations and Questions

Temporary situation: It is true now, but it is not forever.
Time clues: lately these days this month
My husband has been working nights this month.
These days, we've been riding our bikes to work.
Ben hasn't been coming to class lately.
What have you been reading lately?
How long have you been playing the guitar?

A How long question has no for or since. The answer often does: "For two years."

 

Exercise 3 — Temporary Situations and Questions

Write the present perfect continuous form of the verb. Then click to check.

1. My grandmother ________ (go) to a yoga class at the library these days.

has been going — these days = a temporary situation.

2. ________ (you / eat) a lot of fast food lately? Your doctor says your cholesterol is high.

Have you been eating — Question: Have + you + been + verb-ing.

3. We ________ (not / use) the car much this month because gas is so expensive.

haven't been using (or have not been using) — Negative: haven't + been + verb-ing.

4. What ________ (your kids / do) after school these days?

have your kids been doing — Your kids = they, so use have.

5. How long ________ (Ramon / wait) at the bus stop? He looks cold.

has Ramon been waiting — A How long question. Ramon = has.

6. My English ________ (get) better lately because I watch movies with English subtitles.

has been getting — lately = a change happening now.

 

Present Perfect Continuous or Present Perfect?

Present perfect continuousPresent perfect
The activity (maybe not finished)A number, so far, or a finished result
Mia has been folding laundry all afternoon.Mia has folded five baskets.
I've been washing the car. (My shoes are wet.)I've washed the car. (It's clean now.)
Stative verbs (be, know, like, own) do not use -ing here.
I've known about the problem all week. — NOT have been knowing

Exercise 5 — Find the Mistake

Each sentence has one mistake in the CAPITALIZED words. Say the correct present perfect continuous or present perfect form. Then click to check.

1. My kids HAS BEEN ARGUING about the TV remote all evening.

have been arguing — My kids = they, so use have.

2. Your hair is wet. HAVE YOU BEEN SWIM at the pool?

Have you been swimming — Been is followed by verb-ing.

3. These days, Marta HAS WORKING two jobs to pay for school.

has been working — Don't forget been.

4. My uncle HAS BEEN OWNING that old truck all his life.

has owned — Own is a stative verb, so no -ing.

5. Grandpa's face is red from the sun. He HAS BEEN RAKE leaves in the yard all afternoon.

has been raking — Evidence now. Been is followed by verb-ing.

6. The cat next door HAVE BEEN SLEEPING on my car lately. There are paw prints everywhere.

has been sleeping — The cat = it, so use has.

Write the present perfect continuous or present perfect form of the verb. Then click to check.

1. Pablo ________ (drink) four glasses of water this afternoon.

has drunk — A number (four glasses) = present perfect.

2. Why are you so dusty? ________ (you / clean) the attic all morning?

Have you been cleaning — Evidence now (the dust) + all morning.

has been crying — Evidence now: red eyes and wet cheeks.

 

Summary — No For or Since? Look for Clues

UseClueExample
Evidence nowYou see, hear, or smell a resultThe floor is wet. Someone has been mopping.
Excuse or reasonSorry / excuse me + whyExcuse my messy hair. I've been working in the garden.
Complaintagain, !My little brother has been using my headphones again!
Repeated actionall day, all week (no number)The car alarm downstairs has been beeping all week.
