Adverbs of Manner

How do you do it?

Level 2 — Grammar

 

What Is an Adverb of Manner?

An adverb of manner tells HOW someone does something. It describes a verb (an action).

Ask: How?
Hana speaks softly. → How does Hana speak? → softly
The boys laugh loudly. → How do they laugh? → loudly
Kenji paints slowly. → How does Kenji paint? → slowly
My aunt dances beautifully. → How does she dance? → beautifully

Many adverbs of manner end in -ly.

 

Where Does the Adverb Go?

After the verb: verb + adverb
Hana speaks softly.
After the object: verb + object + adverb
Kenji paints the wall slowly.
✓ Kenji paints the wall slowly.
✗ Kenji paints slowly the wall.
Remember: Do not put the adverb between the verb and its object.
 

How to Make an Adverb

Adjective ends in…RuleExamples
most wordsadd -lyslow → slowly, kind → kindly, brave → bravely
consonant + yy → ilynoisy → noisily, lazy → lazily
consonant + lele → lysimple → simply, comfortable → comfortably
special wordsdifferent word or no changegood → well, fast → fast, hard → hard
Watch out: No fastly, no goodly.
 

Exercise 1 — Make the Adverb

Change the word in ( ) to an adverb. Then click to check.

1. Farid carried the eggs ______ to the car. (gentle)

Farid carried the eggs gently to the car. — gentle ends in -le → gently.

2. Grace waved ______ at her friends from the train window. (happy)

Grace waved happily at her friends from the train window. — happy: y → ily.

3. “Good morning! How can I help you?” Olga answered ______. (polite)

Olga answered politely. — Most words: add -ly.

4. My grandmother is 90, but she still hears ______. (good)

She still hears well. — good → well (special word).

5. After the long hike, the boys ate their sandwiches ______. (hungry)

The boys ate their sandwiches hungrily. — hungry: y → ily.

6. The new tour guide spoke ______, so everyone understood her. (clear)

The new tour guide spoke clearly. — Most words: add -ly.

Exercise 4 — Find the Mistake

The CAPITALIZED part has a mistake. Say the correct sentence. Then click to check.

1. Grandpa smiled WARM at his grandchildren.

Grandpa smiled warmly at his grandchildren. — smiled = action → adverb.

2. The bank sent a QUICKLY reply to my email.

The bank sent a quick reply to my email. — It describes a noun (reply) → adjective.

3. The little boy solved the math problem EASYLY.

The little boy solved the math problem easily. — easy: y → ily.

4. The kitchen smells FRESHLY after Hana cleans it.

The kitchen smells fresh after Hana cleans it. — smell is a special verb → adjective.

5. Olga signed CAREFULLY the papers.

Olga signed the papers carefully. (or: Olga carefully signed the papers.) — verb + object + adverb, or adverb before the verb.

6. This new medicine works GOOD. I feel much better.

This new medicine works well. — works = action → adverb. good → well.
