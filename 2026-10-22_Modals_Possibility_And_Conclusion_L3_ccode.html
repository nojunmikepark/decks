Modals of Possibility and Conclusion

Guessing from clues: must · may / might / could · can’t / must not

Level 3 — Modals, Day 3

 

How Sure Are You?

Look at the clue. Then choose a modal for your guess.

Form: subject + modal + base verb
about 95% sure YES → must
about 50% sure (maybe) → may / might / could
about 95% sure NOT → can’t / must not
Her hands are covered in flour. She must be baking.

No to and no -s after the modal: She musts be baking. She must to be baking.

 

must = About 95% Sure

Rule: Use must when a clue makes you almost sure.
There are wet footprints in the hall. Ben must be back from the pool.
The violin teacher is clapping. Her student must play very well.
Two meanings of must:
A rule: Drivers must stop at a red light.
A guess from a clue: Your bag looks heavy. It must be full of books.
 

may / might / could = About 50% Sure

Rule: Use may, might, or could when you are guessing and have no strong clue.
Nobody has answered my email. The answer may come tomorrow.
That restaurant could be crowded on a Friday night.
My brother might not like spicy food.
Watch out: maybe not = may not / might not. could not does NOT mean maybe. It means can’t.
 

can’t / must not = About 95% Sure NOT

Rule: Use can’t or must not when a clue makes you almost sure something is NOT true.
Kim bought that phone yesterday. It can’t be broken already!
The oven is still cold. The cookies must not be ready.

In American English, say must not (not mustn’t) for this kind of guess.

 

Exercise 1 — How Sure? Choose the Modal

Read the clue. Click a, b, c, or d. The correct button turns green; a wrong button turns red and the correct one is highlighted.

1. The hotel hallway smells like fresh paint. Someone ____ be painting the rooms.

2. Our concert tickets say Row Z, the last row. Our seats ____ be close to the stage.

3. Tomás hasn’t checked the mail yet. A letter ____ be there, or maybe nothing came today.

4. The ice on the lake is melting, and the birds are singing again. Spring ____ be coming.

5. My cousin just texted, “Running late!” She ____ be busy at work, or maybe she overslept.

6. Mr. Gómez’s face is red, and he is shouting angrily at the TV. His team ____ be winning.

7. The sea is very rough this morning. The ferry ____ leave late, or maybe it won’t go at all.

8. Everyone in the theater keeps laughing. That movie ____ be very funny.

 

Exercise 2 — Write the Modal

Read the clue. Write must, can’t, may, might, or could to match the meaning in parentheses. Then click to check.

1. The streetlights just came on. It ____ be getting dark. (95% sure)

must — A strong clue = about 95% sure.

2. Chloe’s flight from Denver lands at midnight. She ____ take a taxi home. (50% sure)

might (or may / could) — A guess about later = about 50%.

3. The key I found in the yard doesn’t fit our front door. It ____ be our house key. (95% sure not)

can’t (or cannot / must not / couldn’t / could not) — The key does not fit = about 95% sure NOT.

4. Bruno keeps checking his watch and tapping his foot at the bus stop. He ____ be in a hurry. (95% sure)

must — Two strong clues = about 95% sure.

5. The store is having a big sale. It ____ not have my size. (50% sure)

might (or may) — maybe not = might not / may not (not could not).

6. A big bag of dog food is on our porch, but we don’t have a dog. It ____ be our order. (95% sure not)

can’t (or cannot / must not / couldn’t / could not) — No dog = about 95% sure NOT.

7. The museum is free today, so my sister ____ come with us. (50% sure)

might (or may / could) — A guess, not a sure thing = about 50%.

8. The cat is asleep on the sofa. The noise in the kitchen ____ be the cat. (95% sure not)

can’t (or cannot / must not / couldn’t / could not) — The cat is on the sofa = about 95% sure NOT.
