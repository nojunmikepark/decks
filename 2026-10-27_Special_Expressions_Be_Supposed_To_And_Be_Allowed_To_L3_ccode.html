Grammar · Level 3 · Special Expressions

Supposed To & Allowed To

Rules, plans, permission, and "I suppose"

be + supposed / allowed + to + verb
 

Part 1 · The idea

be supposed to

Use be supposed to for what people expect: a rule, a duty, or a plan.

Drivers are supposed to stop at a red light. (a rule)
I am supposed to pick up my nephew at 3:00. (a plan)
Amir is supposed to lock the office this week. (a duty)

It means "should, because someone says so."

 

Part 1 · The form

am / is / are + supposed to + verb

SubjectYes (+)No (−)Question (?)
II'm supposed to help.I'm not supposed to help.Am I supposed to help?
he / she / itHe's supposed to help.He isn't supposed to help.Is he supposed to help?
you / we / theyThey're supposed to help.They aren't supposed to help.Are they supposed to help?
Watch out: say "supposed" with -d, and use the base verb after to.
 

Part 2 · The past

was / were supposed to

In the past, it often means: that was the plan, but it did not happen.

The pizza was supposed to arrive at 6:00, but it came at 7:15.
We were supposed to go hiking, but it rained all day.
 

Part 3 · A different meaning

I suppose (so)

No be, no -ed. I suppose = I think so, probably. I am not very sure.

"Is this milk still good?" "I suppose so. It smells OK."

It can also mean yes, but I am not happy about it.

"Can I borrow your umbrella?" "I suppose so. Just bring it back!"

I suppose so. = probably yes  ·  I suppose not. = probably no

 

Part 4 · Permission

be allowed to

Use be allowed to when you have permission. A rule or a person says yes.

Yes

Shoppers are allowed to return items for 30 days.

No

You aren't allowed to text while you drive.

Past: was / were allowed to   Question: Am I allowed to ...?
Compare: allowed to = you CAN (permission). supposed to = you SHOULD (people expect it).

Practice 2

Find and Fix

Each sentence has one mistake in CAPITAL letters. Say the correct sentence, then click to check.

1. My cousin is supposed to VISITS us tonight.

My cousin is supposed to visit us tonight. — After to, use the base verb.

2. Are we ALLOWING to use a dictionary on the quiz?

Are we allowed to use a dictionary on the quiz? — be allowed to: allowed, with -ed.

3. The bus driver SUPPOSED to wait for us, but he left.

The bus driver was supposed to wait for us, but he left. — Do not forget be: was supposed to.

4. Customers aren't allowed to TASTING the fruit at this market.

Customers aren't allowed to taste the fruit at this market. — After to, use the base verb.

5. "Are you going to finish that pie?" "I SUPPOSING so."

"Are you going to finish that pie?" "I suppose so." — I suppose so = I think so.

6. We were SUPPOSE to meet at the gym at noon, but I overslept.

We were supposed to meet at the gym at noon, but I overslept. — supposed to: spell it with -d.
