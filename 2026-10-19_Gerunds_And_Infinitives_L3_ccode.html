Gerunds and Infinitives

Gerund or infinitive? The position and the main verb decide.

Level 3 — Grammar

 

Two Forms of a Verb

Gerund = verb + -ing  →  swimming, reading
Infinitive = to + base verb  →  to swim, to read
Mario enjoys swimming at the pool.
Mario decided to swim every week.

Some positions take only a gerund or only an infinitive. After a verb, the MAIN VERB decides.

 

Gerunds: Three Positions

PositionPatternExample
subject of a sentenceS = GerReading before bed helps me sleep.
after a preposition (phrasal verbs too)Prep + GerWe are thinking about getting a dog.
after some verbsV + GerLina avoids eating fried food.
V + Ger verbs: advise · avoid · consider · deny · dislike · enjoy · finish · postpone · practice · quit · suggest
Watch out: She enjoys to swim. → She enjoys swimming.
 

Infinitives: Three Positions

PositionPatternExample
after an adjectiveAdj + InfIt is hard to find parking downtown.
after a noun or pronoun objectN + InfMy boss asked me to stay late.
after other verbsV + InfBen promised to write every week.
V + Inf verbs: agree · ask · choose · come · decide · expect · learn · need · offer · plan · promise · want · wish · would like
Watch out: He decided going home. → He decided to go home.
 

The Noun Rule Beats the Verb Rule

Rule: advise usually takes a gerund. But when a noun or pronoun comes after the verb, use to + verb.
The vet advised walking the dog twice a day. (no object → -ing)
The vet advised us to walk the dog twice a day. (object → to + verb)

N + Inf is a very handy rule. The noun rule is more important than the verb rule!

 

Verbs That Take Both Forms

Rule: These verbs take -ing OR to + verb with no change in meaning.
begin · can't stand · continue · hate · like · love · start
The dog began barking. = The dog began to bark.
Kids love playing outside. = Kids love to play outside.

Careful: would like is NOT in this group. It takes only to + verb.

 

Three More Notes

1. Any tense. The main verb can be in any tense. A progressive verb + a gerund gives two -ing words side by side.
Lisa was avoiding answering my calls.
2. go + -ing. Use go + gerund for activities, in any tense.
We went shopping last night.   Do you want to go swimming tomorrow?
3. Objects. Gerunds and infinitives can have their own objects.
I enjoy playing chess. (chess = object of playing)
She asked me to call her. (me = object of asked; her = object of to call)
 

Negatives: Four Ways

PatternExample
1. not + main verbHe didn't want to leave.   She doesn't like cooking.
2. main verb + not + to + verbThey decided not to wait.
3. main verb + to + not + verb (less common)They decided to not wait.
4. main verb + not + -ing (not common)She suggested not driving in the snow.

Exercise 2 — Which Position?

Write the gerund or the infinitive. Then click to check.

1. ________ (drink) enough water is important in the summer.

Drinking — Subject of the sentence: S = Ger.

2. Thank you for ________ (invite) us to your party.

inviting — After the preposition for: Prep + Ger.

3. It is dangerous ________ (text) while you drive.

to text — After the adjective dangerous: Adj + Inf.

4. On Saturdays, my aunt goes ________ (dance) at the community center.

dancing — go + -ing for an activity.

 

Exercise 3 — Fill in the Blank

1. The baby continued ________ (cry) all night.

crying (or to cry) — Continue takes both forms.

2. Our new employee is learning ________ (make) coffee drinks.

to make — Learn is V + Inf.

3. My cousins agreed ________ (rent) their basement to a student.

to rent — Agree is V + Inf.

4. Ahmed postponed ________ (call) the bank, and now his card is blocked.

calling — Postpone is V + Ger.

5. Our teacher wants us ________ (not / use) our phones in class.

not to use (or to not use) — Want + object + not + to + verb.

 

Exercise 4 — Fix the Mistake
