Grammar · Level 2 · Time Expressions

Just, In, On, At

Talking about time

in July · on Friday · at 4:00 · just left
 

Part 1 · The idea

Big Time, Smaller Time, Exact Time

IN

a big time: a month, a year, a season

ON

one day: a day of the week, a date

AT

an exact time: a clock time

Think: IN = big, ON = a day, AT = a point on the clock.
 

Part 1 · The rules

in / on / at

WordUse it forExamples
inmonths, years, seasons, the morning / afternoon / evening, laterin October, in 2030, in the spring, in five minutes
ondays, dates, special dayson Friday, on March 3, on Mother’s Day
atclock times, noon, nightat 4:00, at noon, at night
Watch out: in the morning, in the evening, but at night.
 

Part 2 · in

in = a big time

The new mall opens in October.
My nephew will finish college in 2030.
The park is beautiful in the spring.

in + amount of time = later, after that time.

Dinner will be ready in five minutes.
 

Part 2 · on

on = one day

I do my laundry on Friday.
Her baby was born on March 3.
We call Grandma on Mother’s Day.

A day of the week, a date, or a special day: on.

 

Part 2 · at

at = an exact time

The pool opens at 4:00.
Let’s have a picnic at noon.
Owls hunt at night.

A clock time, noon, midnight, or night: at.

Part 3 · just

just = a very short time ago

Use just for something that happened a moment ago.

Lena just sat down. (She sat down one minute ago.)
The game just started. (It started a moment ago.)
Where? Put just before the past verb: subject + just + past verb.
