Comparative Adjectives

Compare two people, places, or things

Level 2 — Grammar

 

What is a comparative adjective?

We use a comparative adjective to compare two people, places, or things.

Pattern: A + is + adjective-er + than + B
Pattern: A + is + more + adjective + than + B
My car is older than your car.
This book is easier than that book.
Tokyo is larger than Seoul.
Your garden is more beautiful than my garden.
Watch out: The word is than (t-h-a-n). Not then. Not that.
 

Step 1 — Count the syllables

Remember last class? Clap the beats. The number of syllables tells you the rule.

Short word (1 syllable) → -er. Long word (3+ syllables) → more. Two syllables? Look at the last letter.

 

Rule 1 — One syllable: add -er

1 syllable: adjective + -er
long → longer  ·  cool → cooler
The Nile is longer than the Amazon.

Two spelling changes

Ends in -e: add only -r  →  large → larger, close → closer
Consonant-vowel-consonant: double the last letter + -er  →  wet → wetter, sad → sadder
Quick check: w-e-t = consonant, vowel, consonant → double the t: wetter. But cool → cooler (two vowels, no double). And never double w, x, or y: new → newer.
 

Rule 2 — Two syllables ending in -y: y → i + -er

2 syllables, ends in -y: change y to i, then add -er
happy → happier  ·  lucky → luckier  ·  easy → easier
Maria is happier than she was last year.
This puzzle is easier than that puzzle.

Say it: hap-py → hap-pi-er. The sound is the same. Only the spelling changes.

 

Rule 3 — Long adjectives: more + adjective

2 syllables with no -y, or 3 or more syllables: more + adjective
careful → more careful  ·  famous → more famous  ·  beautiful → more beautiful
A nurse is more careful than a child.
Hollywood is more famous than Glendale.

Irregular adjectives — learn them

good → better  ·  bad → worse
Watch out: Use -er OR more. Never both. NOT more longer. NOT more happier.

Exercise 3 — Find the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Say the correct sentence. Then click to check.

1. This rope is more stronger than that rope.

This rope is stronger than that rope. — -er OR more, never both.

2. My uncle's truck is uglyer than my aunt's car.

My uncle's truck is uglier than my aunt's car. — ends in -y: y → i + -er.

3. Honey is sweet than sugar.

Honey is sweeter than sugar. — the adjective needs -er before than.

4. Health is more important then money.

Health is more important than money. — than, not then.

5. My room is cleaner that your room.

My room is cleaner than your room. — than, not that.

6. Pasta is deliciouser than rice.

Pasta is more delicious than rice. — de-li-cious: 3 syllables: more + adjective.
