Comparative Adverbs of Manner

Compare HOW two people or things do something.

Level 2 — Grammar Review

 

Core Idea

An adverb of manner tells HOW someone does something. It comes after an action verb.

Kate is a clear speaker. (adjective — describes a person or thing)
Kate speaks clearly. (adverb — tells HOW she speaks)
Compare two people: action verb + comparative adverb + than
Kate speaks more clearly than Paul.

Look for than. It tells you: compare two people or two things.

 

How to Make the Comparative

AdverbComparativeExample
most -ly adverbsmore + adverb + thanslowly → more slowly than
fast, hardadverb + -er + thanfast → faster than
hard → harder than
irregulara new word + thanwell → better than
badly → worse than
Watch out: Use only one form: NOT more faster. Do not add -er to a -ly adverb: NOT slowlier.
 

Exercise 1 — more + -ly adverb

Use the word in ( ). Say the comparative form. Then click to check.

1. Last night it rained ____ than the night before. Water came into our basement. (heavily)

more heavily — -ly adverb → more + adverb.

2. The twins played ____ at the beach than at home. They laughed all afternoon. (happily)

more happily — -ly adverb → more + adverb. NOT happilier.

3. Ahmed greeted the guests ____ than his brother. He hugged everyone and smiled. (warmly)

more warmly — -ly adverb → more + adverb.

4. Please close the door ____ next time. Grandpa is taking a nap. (gently)

more gently — -ly adverb → more + adverb.

5. Victor spoke ____ than his coworker during the fire drill. He helped everyone walk outside. (calmly)

more calmly — -ly adverb → more + adverb.

6. The young firefighter acted ____ than the older ones. He ran back into the burning house. (bravely)

more bravely — -ly adverb → more + adverb.

 

Special Adverbs: fast, hard, well, badly

These four adverbs do NOT use more.

fast → faster: A plane flies faster than a bird.
hard → harder: Tomas kicks the ball harder than his friend.
well → better: A cat sees better at night than a person.
badly → worse: I slept worse on the plane than in my bed.
Careful: hardly means "almost not." It is NOT the comparative of hard.

Exercise 4 — Fix the Mistake

The CAPITALIZED word or words are wrong. Say the correct word or words. Then click to check.

1. Anna types MORE FASTLY than her manager.

faster — fast → faster. There is no word fastly.

2. The kids in my class behave WORSER than last year.

worse — badly → worse. Do not add -er.

3. The new dentist cleans teeth MORE GENTLE than the old dentist.

more gently — cleans is an action verb, so use the adverb.

4. My neighbor practices the guitar HARDLY than me.

harder — hard → harder. hardly means "almost not."

5. Dad drove MORE CALM than Mom in the heavy traffic.

more calmly — drove is an action verb, so use the adverb.

6. I play the drums MORE WELL than my cousin.

better — well → better (a new word).

 

Exercise 5 — Say It Another Way

Change the sentence. Use the action verb and a comparative adverb. Then click to check.

Hugo is a clearer speaker than Max. → Hugo speaks more clearly than Max.

1. Luis is a better cook than his brother. → Luis ____ than his brother.

Luis cooks better than his brother. — good cook → cooks well → cooks better.

2. Emma is a faster reader than me. → Emma ____ than me.

Emma reads faster than me. — fast → faster.

3. Jun is a harder worker than his roommate. → Jun ____ than his roommate.

Jun works harder than his roommate. — hard → harder.

4. Tara is a more honest player than Sam. → Tara ____ than Sam.

Tara plays more honestly than Sam. — honest → honestly → more honestly.

5. My neighbor is a worse singer than his wife. → My neighbor ____ than his wife.

My neighbor sings worse than his wife. — bad singer → sings badly → sings worse.

6. Yasmin is a more confident swimmer than her brother. → Yasmin ____ than her brother.

Yasmin swims more confidently than her brother. — confident → confidently → more confidently.
