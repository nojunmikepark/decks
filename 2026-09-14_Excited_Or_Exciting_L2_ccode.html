Excited or Exciting?

-ED and -ING Adjectives

Level 2 — Grammar Lesson

 

One Verb, Two Adjectives

The verb excite makes two adjectives: excited and exciting.

-ED = how a person FEELS
-ING = the thing that MAKES the feeling
The game is exciting. (the thing)
The fans are excited. (the people)

Both words are correct. They have different meanings.

 

The Thing or the Person?

The thing (-ING)The person (-ED)
The roller coaster is exciting.Maria is excited.
It was a boring meeting.The workers were bored.
The hike up the mountain was tiring.The hikers were tired.
The story is interesting.The kids are interested.
A thing cannot feel.
✗ The concert was excited.
✓ The concert was exciting.
 

Be Careful with People

A person can be -ED or -ING. The meaning is different.

Mr. Lee is bored. = Mr. Lee feels bored.
Mr. Lee is boring. = Other people feel bored when he talks.
In class, say: “I am bored.”
“I am boring” means you make other people bored!

Exercise 2 — Fix the Mistake

The CAPITALIZED word is wrong. Say the correct word, then click to check.

1. The museum was very INTERESTED.

interesting — A museum cannot feel.

2. My grandma is BORING because she has nothing to do.

bored — Grandma feels it.

3. Carrying heavy boxes all day is TIRED.

tiring — Carrying boxes is a thing, not a person.

4. I am so EXCITING! I start my new job tomorrow.

excited — I feel it.

5. The end of the story was SURPRISED.

surprising — A story cannot feel.

6. My cousin was CONFUSING after he read the long email.

confused — My cousin feels it.

 

Summary — How to Choose
