Grammar · Level 2

Past Continuous

What were you doing?

was / were + -ing
 

Part 1 · The idea

An Action in the Middle

The past continuous shows an action that was in progress at a time in the past.

At 8:00 p.m. yesterday, Omar was ironing his shirts.

At 8:00 he did not start and he did not finish. He was in the middle.

 

Part 2 · The form

was / were + verb-ing

SubjectYes (+)No (−)Question (?)
II was singing.I wasn't singing.Was I singing?
he / she / itShe was singing.She wasn't singing.Was she singing?
you / we / theyThey were singing.They weren't singing.Were they singing?
Short answers: Yes, they were.   No, they weren't.

wasn't = was not  ·  weren't = were not

 

Part 2 · Spelling

Adding -ing

Most verbs

add -ing
talk → talking
study → studying

Ends in one -e (not -ie)

drop the e
smile → smiling
bike → biking

consonant + vowel + consonant

short verbs: double the last letter (not w, x, y)
shop → shopping
stop → stopping

Ends in -ie

ie → y
tie → tying

Part 3 · Two actions

Long Action + Short Action

The long action uses the past continuous. The short action uses the simple past.

long action + when + short action

While + long action, short action
Dina was knitting a scarf when she heard a strange noise.
While we were vacuuming, the baby woke up.
Tip: while goes with the long action. when usually goes with the short action.

Practice 3

Find and Fix

Each sentence has one mistake in CAPITAL letters. Keep the sentence in the past. Say the correct sentence, then click to check.

1. My parents WAS dancing at the wedding.

My parents were dancing at the wedding. — My parents = they: were.

2. Alex WERE washing the car at 4:00.

Alex was washing the car at 4:00. — Alex = he: was.

3. I was READ a magazine on the train.

I was reading a magazine on the train. — was / were + verb-ing.

4. WERE your sister working yesterday afternoon?

Was your sister working yesterday afternoon? — Your sister = she: Was ... ?

5. The children WASN'T listening to the teacher.

The children weren't listening to the teacher. — The children = they: weren't.

6. Ben was RUNING to catch the bus.

Ben was running to catch the bus. — run: consonant + vowel + consonant, so double the n: running.
