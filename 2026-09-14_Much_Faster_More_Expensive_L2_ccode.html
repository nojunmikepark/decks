Much Faster, More Expensive

Comparatives: -er or more / less, and where much goes

Level 2 — Grammar

 

Look at These Three Sentences

All three compare two things. Look at the colored part.

She is faster than her father.
That car is more expensive than those shoes.
The pencil is less expensive than the car.

Why faster but more expensive? Two questions answer everything:

1. How many syllables is the adjective?
2. Is the difference big or small? (That tells you if you need much.)

Rule 2 — Where Does "much" Go?

[A] + is + much + faster / more expensive / less expensive + than + [B]

much goes right before the comparative. Nowhere else.

Wrong: The bus is more faster than the train.
Wrong: The bus is faster much than the train.
Wrong: The bus is much expensiver than the train.

Use much only for a BIG difference. No much = a normal or small difference.

Small difference — no much: The coffee is more expensive than the pencil. (only $2)
Big difference — use much: A car is much more expensive than a pair of shoes. ($19,940!)
Big difference with less: The pencil is much less expensive than the bike.
Big difference with speed: Riding a bike is much faster than walking.
 

Build It Step by Step

Start: The laptop is expensive. Say each step out loud, then click.

Step 1. Make it a comparative. Compare it to the phone.

The laptop is more expensive than the phone. ex-pen-sive = 3 syllables, so more, not -er.

Step 2. The difference is big. Add much.

The laptop is much more expensive than the phone. much goes right before more.

Step 3. Now flip it. Start with the phone and use less.

The phone is less expensive than the laptop. Same facts, opposite direction.

Step 4. Add much to the flipped sentence.

The phone is much less expensive than the laptop. much + less + adjective.
