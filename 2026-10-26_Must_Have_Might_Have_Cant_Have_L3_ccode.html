Must Have, Might Have and Can’t Have

guessing about the past

Level 3 — Grammar Review

 

Now or Past?

You already use must / might / can’t to guess about now. To guess about the past, add have + the past participle.

TimeFormExample
nowmust + base verbI can hear their TV. They must be home.
pastmust have + past participleTheir lights were on all night. They must have stayed up late.

We see a clue now, and we guess what happened before.

 

must have = 95% Sure It Happened

must have + past participle = I have a strong clue. I’m almost sure it happened.
There’s snow on every car. It must have snowed last night.
Rafa knows the whole city. He must have lived here a long time.
Watch out: use the past participle, not the simple past. must have flew → must have flown
 

might / may / could have = 50% Sure

might have / may have / could have + past participle = maybe it happened. I’m not sure.
Jin is late. He might have missed the train.
Lola looks sleepy. She may have slept in, or maybe she was up late.
Tip: might have, may have and could have all mean the same thing here.
 

can’t have = 95% Sure It Did NOT Happen

can’t have / couldn’t have + past participle = I’m almost sure it did not happen. It’s impossible.
The kids can’t have built this robot alone. It’s too hard for them.
Mateo couldn’t have known about the surprise. Nobody told him.
Watch out: always have, never has. She can’t has → She can’t have
 

How Sure Are You?

How sure?FormClue words
95% yesmust havea strong clue: “I’m sure”
50%might / may / could have“maybe,” “I’m not sure,” “or maybe …”
95% nocan’t / couldn’t have“impossible,” “no way”

Exercise 2 — How Sure Are You?

Read the clue. Say must have, might have, or can’t have + the past participle. Then click to check.

1. Lucas got the highest score in the class. He ____ really hard. (study)

2. Ms. Ruiz isn’t at her desk. She ____ an early lunch, or maybe she’s in a meeting. (have)

might have had (or may have had / could have had) — “or maybe” = 50% sure.

3. Arturo ____ the fence last week. He was in the hospital all week. (paint)

can’t have painted (or couldn’t have painted / must not have painted) — in the hospital = impossible.

4. There’s a new scratch on my car door. Someone ____ it. (hit)

must have hit (or must’ve hit) — the scratch is a strong clue. hit → hit → hit.

5. Tom ____ this whole book last night. It has 900 pages. (read)

can’t have read (or couldn’t have read / must not have read) — 900 pages in one night = impossible. read → read → read.

6. Juan wasn’t in class today. He ____ a day off, or maybe he was sick. (take)

might have taken (or may have taken / could have taken) — “or maybe” = 50% sure. take → took → taken.

7. The street is covered with leaves and broken branches. There ____ a big storm last night. (be)

must have been (or must’ve been) — a strong clue: 95% sure. be → was → been.

8. Kim ____ my birthday present. She didn’t even know about my birthday. (buy)

can’t have bought (or couldn’t have bought / must not have bought) — she didn’t know = impossible. buy → bought.

 

Exercise 3 — Now or Past?

Say must + base verb (now) or must have + past participle (past). Then click to check.

1. Our neighbors have four cars and a pool. They ____ very rich. (must / be)

must be — a guess about now: must + base verb.

2. The kids are eating ice cream. The ice cream truck ____ by already. (must / come)

must have come (or must’ve come) — it came before now: must have + past participle. come → came → come.

3. My car is clean and shiny. My son ____ it this morning. (must / wash)

must have washed (or must’ve washed) — this morning is past.

4. Mr. Patel speaks five languages. He ____ very smart. (must / be)

must be — a guess about now: must + base verb.

5. Mariam is wearing a heavy coat inside. She ____ cold right now. (must / feel)

must feel — right now: must + base verb.

6. The restaurant was dark and empty at 8:00 last night. It ____ early. (must / close)

must have closed (or must’ve closed) — last night is past.

 

Exercise 4 — Fix the Mistake

Each sentence has one mistake in the CAPITALIZED word. Say the correct word. Then click to check.

1. Wei must have MAKE a mistake. The total on the bill is wrong.

made — make → made → made. Use the past participle.

2. The kids might HAS taken the TV remote to their room.

have — might + have, never has.

3. Oscar must have CHOSE this restaurant. It’s his favorite.

chosen — choose → chose → chosen.

4. Somebody must have GIVE the dog a bath. He smells great.

given — give → gave → given.

5. My brother must HAD fixed the sink. It doesn’t leak anymore.

have — must + have, never had.

6. Our guests can’t have ATE much soup. The pot is still full.

eaten — eat → ate → eaten.

 

Summary — Main Takeaways
