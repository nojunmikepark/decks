Before we compare

Adjective or Adverb?

 

The problem

Two kinds of comparatives

We compare things with adjectives. We compare actions with adverbs.

Adjective

The second customer was ruder than the first.

Adverb

The second customer spoke more rudely than the first.

Always first: what does the word describe?
 

Step 1

Adjective or adverb?

It describes ...Example
Adjectivea noun, or it comes after be / look / feel / taste / seemThis tea is stronger.
You look happier today.
Adverban action verb (How?)She shouts more loudly.
The old dog walks more slowly now.

Ask: Which word does it go with? A noun or be/look/feel → adjective. An action → adverb.

 

Step 2

Which rule?

RuleAdjectivesAdverbs
+ -er1 syllable: cold → colder
thin → thinner (double the last letter)		same as the adjective: fast → faster
y → -ier2 syllables + y: tiny → tinier—
more + word2+ syllables: important → more important-ly adverbs: slowly → more slowly
irregulargood → better, bad → worse, far → fartherwell → better, badly → worse, far → farther
 

Watch out

Three traps

1. better = good and well. worse = bad and badly.
2. -ly adverbs never take -er: more slowly, not slowlier.
3. Use -er or more, never both: colder, not more colder.

Practice 3 · 1–2

Find and Fix

Each sentence has one mistake in CAPITAL letters. Say the correct sentence, then click to check.

1. Ella greets customers MORE POLITE than her coworker.

Ella greets customers more politely than her coworker. — greets = action verb → adverb: more politely.

2. The second test was MORE HARD than the first test.

The second test was harder than the first test. — hard = 1 syllable → harder. No more.

3. My grandma cooks MORE WELL than a chef.

My grandma cooks better than a chef. — well → better (irregular).

4. Our city is MORE NOISY at night than in the morning.

Our city is noisier at night than in the morning. — noisy ends in -y → noisier.

 

Practice 3 · 5–6

Find and Fix

Each sentence has one mistake in CAPITAL letters. Say the correct sentence, then click to check.

5. After the long drive, my back feels MORE BAD.

After the long drive, my back feels worse. — feels + adjective. bad → worse (irregular).

6. The kids play MORE HAPPY at the park than at home.

The kids play more happily at the park than at home. — play = action verb → adverb: more happily.
