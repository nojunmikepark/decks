Comparatives and Superlatives 3

Use a comparative to compare two things. Use a superlative to compare three or more things.

Grammar Notes

1. One-syllable adjectives

Add -er (comparative) and -est (superlative).

long → longer than → the longest

  • Ends in e: add -r / -st: safe → safer → the safest
  • Ends in 1 consonant + 1 vowel + 1 consonant (not w, x, or y): double the last letter: fat → fatter → the fattest

2. Adjectives ending in consonant + y

Change y to i, then add -er / -est.

easy → easier → the easiest

3. Other adjectives with 2 or more syllables

Use more and the most.

exciting → more exciting → the most exciting

4. Irregular adjectives

  • good → better → the best
  • bad → worse → the worst
  • far → farther → the farthest

5. As ... as

Two things are the same: as + adjective + as. Not the same: not as ... as.

My coat is as long as your coat. My coat is not as long as your coat.

6. Comparative adverbs (How?)

  • Most adverbs with -ly: use more: slowly → more slowly
  • Some adverbs look like adjectives. Add -er: fast → faster
  • Irregular: well → better, badly → worse

He speaks more slowly than his boss.

Tip: Comparatives use than. Superlatives use the and often of all or in + a place or group.
