First Conditional

if + simple present, will + base verb

Level 3 — Grammar Practice

 

What Is the First Conditional?

We use it for a real possibility in the future. Maybe it will happen, maybe not.

If + simple present,   will + base verb
If Rita receives the scholarship, she will become a nurse.

The if-clause is the condition. The other clause is the result.

 

Two Orders, One Meaning

If-clause FIRST → use a comma.
Result FIRST → no comma.
If the elevator breaks, we will use the stairs.
We will use the stairs if the elevator breaks.

In the result, be going to, can, may, and might also work.

 

Negatives

If-clause: don't / doesn't + base verb
Result: won't + base verb
If the museum doesn't open on time, we won't see the dinosaur show.
 

Watch Out

No will after if: If my brother will lend me his truck, I will move the couch.
→ If my brother lends me his truck, I will move the couch.
Base verb after will: She will drives. → She will drive.

Exercise 2 — Fill in the Blank

Use the verb in parentheses. Use the simple present or will. Then click to check.

1. If Hana ____ (feel) better tomorrow, she will go back to work.

2. We ____ (bake) cookies if the kids get home early tomorrow.

3. If the neighbors ____ (not turn) down the music, I will knock on their door.

4. If you ____ (lose) your ticket, you will pay again.

lose — If-clause: simple present.

5. Mr. Cho ____ (not be) at the party if his daughter has a fever.

won't be (or will not be / isn't going to be) — Negative result: won't + base verb.

6. If the bookstore ____ (have) the book, I will buy it this week.

has — If-clause: simple present.

7. If our team ____ (score) a goal, the crowd will go wild.

scores — If-clause: simple present.

8. Your shoes ____ (be) muddy if you walk across that field.

will be (or are going to be) — Result: will + base verb.

 

Exercise 3 — Fix the Mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Correct the CAPITALIZED word or words. Then click to check.

1. If Ali WILL MISS the last train, he will sleep at his cousin's place.

misses — No will after if: If Ali misses the last train …

2. If the shop has my size, I BUY the boots.

will buy (or am going to buy / can buy / may buy / might buy) — Result: will + base verb.

3. If Pam DON'T like the color, she will return the dress.

doesn't (or does not) — She → doesn't + base verb.

4. They will WATCHES the fireworks if the sky is clear.

watch — After will, use the base verb.

5. If you ASKED nicely, he will help you.

ask — If-clause: simple present.

6. We WOULD NOT need jackets if the sun comes out.

will not (or won't / aren't going to / may not / might not) — Result: will not (won't) + base verb.
