Present Perfect Continuous

have / has + been + verb-ing. Use it for an action that started in the past and is still going on, or one that just stopped and you can see the result.

Grammar Notes

1. Form

I / you / we / they + have been + verb-ing

he / she / it + has been + verb-ing

He has been knitting a scarf.
They haven't been answering their emails.
Have they been resting? Yes, they have.

2. Still going on

The action started in the past and is still happening. Use for, since, all day, or How long…?

He has been knitting that scarf for two weeks.
How long have they been resting? Since noon.

3. Just stopped: you can see the result

Your shirt is wet. Have you been washing the car?

4. These days

Use lately or these days for something true now but not forever.

We have been walking more these days.

5. Present perfect or present perfect continuous?

  • A number, or a finished result: present perfect. I have written three emails.
  • The activity and how long: present perfect continuous. I have been writing emails all morning.
  • Stative verbs (like, believe, need, belong) are not used in the continuous form.

6. -ing spelling

  • Most verbs: + -ing (read → reading)
  • Silent -e: drop the e (make → making)
  • -ie: change to y (lie → lying)
  • One-syllable verb that ends consonant + vowel + consonant: double the last letter (sit → sitting). Not w, x, or y (snow → snowing).
