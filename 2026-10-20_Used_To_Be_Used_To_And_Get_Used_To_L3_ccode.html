Used To, Be Used To, and Get Used To

past habits · things that are normal · things becoming normal

Level 3 — Grammar

 

Three Different Meanings

FormMeaningExample
used to + verba past habit; not true nowGrace used to sing in a choir.
be used to + noun / verb-ingnormal for you; not newPaul is a mail carrier. He is used to rainy days.
get used to + noun / verb-ingbecoming normal; it was new or hardNina is getting used to her new braces.

The words look the same, but the meanings are different.

 

Used To + Verb: Past Habits

Rule: used to + base verb = something happened again and again in the past, or was true in the past. It is not true now.
Grace used to sing in a choir. (She doesn't now.)
Negative: Grace didn't use to sing alone.
Question: Did Grace use to sing in a choir?

After did / didn't, write use to (no d).

 

Be Used To: Normal for You

Rule: be used to + noun or be used to + verb-ing = it is normal for you. It is not new or strange.
Paul is used to rainy days.
The pilots are used to flying at night.
I wasn't used to snow before I moved to Boston.

Change be for the subject and the time: am / is / are / was / were.

 

Get Used To: Becoming Normal

Rule: get used to + noun or get used to + verb-ing = something new or hard is slowly becoming normal.
Nina is getting used to her new braces.
After two weeks, Nina got used to them.
Don't worry. You will get used to speaking in front of the class.

Change get for the time: get / gets / got / is getting / will get.

 

Watch Out: To + What?

used tobe used to / get used to
+ base verb+ noun or verb-ing
I used to surf.I am used to surfing.
Watch out: I am used to surf. → I am used to surfing.
Watch out: Did you used to surf? → Did you use to surf?
