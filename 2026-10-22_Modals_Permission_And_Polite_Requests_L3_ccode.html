Modals: Permission and Polite Requests

May I …? · Could I …? · Could you …? · Would you …?

Level 3 — Asking Politely

 

Two Ways to Ask Politely

Permission = I want to do something  →  May I / Could I + base verb …?
Request = I want YOU to do something  →  Could you / Would you + base verb …?
Knocking on a door: May I come in?
The delivery driver: Would you sign here, please?

Look at the subject. I = permission. you = request.

 

Asking for Permission

Rule: May I or Could I + base verb. May is the most formal. Could is polite too.
At the park: Could I pet your dog?
At a friend’s house: May I come in?
In a friend’s car: Could I turn on the radio?
Watch out: Would I come in? → May I come in?
 

Polite Requests

Rule: Could you or Would you + base verb. Add please to be extra polite.
To a clerk: Could you write that down for me?
To a friend: Would you keep an eye on my bag for a minute?
Watch out: May you sign here? → Would you sign here?  ·  Could you to write it? → Could you write it?
 

I or You? And What About Can?

QuestionWho does it?Meaning
Could I drive?I drive.permission
Could you drive?You drive.request
May I pour the tea?I pour it.permission
Would you pour the tea?You pour it.request

Can I …? / Can you …? are fine with friends, but they are less polite. In today’s exercises, use may, could, or would.

 

Polite Answers

Yes: Sure. / Of course. / No problem.
No: I’m sorry, but … (give a reason)
A: Could I pet your dog?   B: Sure! He’s very friendly.
A: Would you sign here, please?   B: Of course.
A: May I come in?   B: I’m sorry, but the meeting has started.

Exercise 2 — Fill in the Blank

Write the polite words for the meaning in parentheses. Some blanks also need I or you. Use may, could, or would (not can). Then click to check.

1. At the hair salon: “____ wash my hair first? (request)”

Could you (or Would you) — You want the stylist to do it.

2. At the movie theater: “____ I bring my coffee inside? (permission)”

May (or Could) — Subject I = permission.

3. To a neighbor: “There is a lot of snow on my sidewalk. ____ use your shovel? (permission)”

May I (or Could I) — You want to use it: permission with I.

4. At the post office: “____ weigh this package for me? (request)”

Could you (or Would you) — You want the clerk to do it.

5. In class: “I finished my group’s poster. ____ I hang it on the wall? (permission)”

May (or Could) — Subject I = permission.

6. On the phone: “Hello, ____ I reserve a table for Friday night? (permission)”

May (or Could) — Subject I = permission.

7. At a hardware store: “____ cut this wood in half for me? (request)”

Could you (or Would you) — You want the worker to do it.

8. To a neighbor: “Your tree is hanging over my roof. ____ you trim it this weekend? (request)”

Could (or Would) — Subject you = request.
