Should Have and Shouldn’t Have

regret and criticism about the past

Level 3 — Grammar Review

 

Now or Past?

You already know should for advice. To talk about the past, add have + the past participle.

TimeFormExample
now / futureshould + base verbIt’s cold. You should wear a jacket.
pastshould have + past participleIt was cold yesterday. You should have worn a jacket.

Yesterday is over. We can’t change it, so should have talks about a past mistake.

 

should have = a Good Idea That Didn’t Happen

should have + past participle = it was a good idea, but it did not happen.
I failed my road test. I should have practiced more.
Our cat ran outside. Someone should have closed the door.
Watch out: use the past participle, not the simple past. should have drove → should have driven
 

shouldn’t have = a Bad Idea That Happened

shouldn’t have + past participle = it was a bad idea, but it did happen.
I shouldn’t have yelled at my brother. Now he won’t talk to me.
They shouldn’t have painted the bedroom black. It’s so dark.
Watch out: always have, never has. She shouldn’t has → She shouldn’t have
 

Regret or Criticism?

Who?MeaningExample
I / weregret: I feel bad about my choiceI should have called you.
you / he / she / theycriticism: their choice was wrongYou should have invited me! · He shouldn’t have lied.
 

Saying It

We writeWe say
should haveshould’ve (“SHOOD-uv”)
shouldn’t have“SHOOD-nt-uv”

“Should’ve” sounds like “should of,” but we never write should of.

Exercise 3 — Now or Past?

Say should + base verb (now / future) or should have + past participle (past). Then click to check.

1. Your sister’s wedding is on Saturday. You ____ your speech in front of a mirror. (should / rehearse)

should rehearse — the wedding is in the future: should + base verb.

2. Kwame’s new apartment is very noisy at night. He ____ it at night before he signed the lease. (should / visit)

should have visited (or should’ve visited) — he signed the lease in the past.

3. Last winter, our pipes froze and broke. We ____ the heat on while we were away. (should / leave)

should have left (or should’ve left) — last winter is past. leave → left → left.

4. Your library books are due next Tuesday. You ____ them back this weekend. (should / take)

should take — this weekend is in the future: should + base verb.

5. Imani’s package went to her old address last month. She ____ her address on the website before she ordered it. (should / change)

should have changed (or should’ve changed) — she ordered it in the past, before the package went out.

6. Your hands are shaking right now. You ____ down for a few minutes. (should / sit)

should sit — advice for right now: should + base verb.

 

Exercise 4 — Fix the Mistake

Each sentence has one mistake in the CAPITALIZED word. Say the correct word. Then click to check.

1. Ahmed should have SAW the price before he ordered the lobster.

seen — see → saw → seen. Use the past participle.

2. The kids shouldn’t have OPEN their presents before Grandma arrived.

opened — should have + past participle: opened.

3. I should HAD ordered the chicken. This fish is terrible.

have — should + have, never had.

4. You should OF asked me for a ride!

have — We say “should’ve,” but we write should have.

5. Sofia shouldn’t have THREW away the receipt. Now she can’t return the dress.

thrown — throw → threw → thrown.

6. We should have BEGAN the project last week. It’s due tomorrow.

begun — begin → began → begun.

 

Summary — Main Takeaways

FormMeaningExample
should + base verbadvice for now or the futureYou should bring water on the hike tomorrow.
should have + past participlepast: a good idea that didn’t happenI should have written down her number.
shouldn’t have + past participlepast: a bad idea that happenedHe shouldn’t have driven in the snow.

I / we = regret. You / he / she / they = criticism. Always have, never has or of.
