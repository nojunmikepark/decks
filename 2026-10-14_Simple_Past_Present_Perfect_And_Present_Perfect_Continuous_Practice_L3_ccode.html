Simple Past, Present Perfect, or Present Perfect Continuous?

A finished time takes the simple past. Up to now takes the present perfect. Still going on takes the present perfect continuous.

Grammar Notes

1. Simple past

verb + -ed, or the irregular past form

A finished action at a finished time: yesterday, last week, two days ago, in 2019

We traveled to Peru in 2019.
She didn't cook last night.

2. Present perfect

have / has + past participle

  • How many times, or how much, up to now (so far)
  • Stative verbs (understand, belong, need) with for / since

We have traveled to Peru twice.
This bike has belonged to my family since 1990.

3. Present perfect continuous

have / has + been + verb-ing

An action that started in the past and is still going on: for the past …, since, How long …?, lately

We have been traveling for three weeks, and we are still in Peru.

4. Watch out

  • since + a starting time: the main verb is not the simple past. I have felt better since my surgery, not I felt better since my surgery. (The since part itself can be simple past: since she was a child.)
  • Stative verbs usually do not take the continuous form.
