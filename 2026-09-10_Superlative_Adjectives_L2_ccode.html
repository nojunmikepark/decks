Superlative Adjectives

Compare three or more people, places, or things

Level 2 — Grammar

 

What is a superlative adjective?

Last class: a comparative compares two things. Today: a superlative adjective picks the number one in a group of three or more.

Pattern: A + is + the + adjective-est + in / of + group
Pattern: A + is + the most + adjective + in / of + group
Bus 33 is the slowest bus in the city.
Today is the wettest day of the year.
Rome is the most beautiful city in Italy.
Watch out: A superlative always has the in front. NOT Rome is most beautiful city. After the superlative, say the group: in the city, of the year, in my family.
 

Step 1 — Count the syllables (again!)

Same first step as comparatives. Clap the beats. The number of syllables tells you the rule.

SyllablesRuleExample
1 syllablethe + adjective + -estold → the oldest
1 syllable, ends in -ethe + adjective + -stlarge → the largest
1 syllable, consonant-vowel-consonantthe + double the last letter + -estwet → the wettest
2 syllables, ends in -ythe + change y → i + -esteasy → the easiest
2 syllables, no -ythe most + adjectivecheerful → the most cheerful
3 or more syllablesthe most + adjectivebeautiful → the most beautiful
irregularnew wordgood → the best, bad → the worst

Comparative -er → superlative -est. Comparative more → superlative the most. Same syllable rules, new ending.

 

Rule 1 — One syllable: the + -est

1 syllable: the + adjective + -est
fast → the fastest  ·  cool → the coolest
The cheetah is the fastest animal in the world.

Two spelling changes — the same as comparatives

Ends in -e: add only -st  →  safe → the safest, close → the closest
Consonant-vowel-consonant: double the last letter + -est  →  wet → the wettest, fit → the fittest
Quick check: w-e-t = consonant, vowel, consonant → double the t: the wettest. But cool → the coolest (two vowels, no double). And never double w, x, or y: slow → the slowest.
 

Rule 2 — Two syllables ending in -y: the + y → i + -est

2 syllables, ends in -y: the + change y to i, then add -est
lucky → the luckiest  ·  easy → the easiest  ·  hungry → the hungriest
Maria is the luckiest person in the office.
This is the easiest exercise in the book.

Say it: luck-y → luck-i-est. Three beats now. Only the spelling of the y changes.

 

Rule 3 — Long adjectives: the most + adjective

2 syllables with no -y, or 3 or more syllables: the most + adjective
careful → the most careful  ·  famous → the most famous  ·  beautiful → the most beautiful
A nurse is the most careful person in the hospital.
Hollywood is the most famous neighborhood in Los Angeles.

Irregular adjectives — learn them

good → better → the best  ·  bad → worse → the worst
Watch out: Use -est OR the most. Never both. NOT the most fastest. NOT the most luckiest.

Exercise 3 — Find the mistake

Each sentence has one mistake. Say the correct sentence. Then click to check.

1. The most quickest way to school is the bus.

The quickest way to school is the bus. — -est OR the most, never both.

2. Who has the noisyest baby?

Who has the noisiest baby? — ends in -y: y → i + -est.

3. I read the interestingest book last week.

I read the most interesting book last week. — in-ter-est-ing: 4 syllables: the most + adjective.

4. Saturday night was most romantic night of my life.

Saturday night was the most romantic night of my life. — a superlative always has the.

5. Mia is the prettiest than all her sisters.

Mia is the prettiest of all her sisters. — a superlative takes of / in, never than.

6. Ben looks the sadest in the photo.

Ben looks the saddest in the photo. — s-a-d: consonant-vowel-consonant: double the d + -est.
