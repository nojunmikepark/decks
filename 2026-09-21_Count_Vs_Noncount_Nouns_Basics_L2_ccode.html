Count vs. Noncount Nouns

The Basics

Level 2 — Grammar Practice

 

What Is a Noun?

A noun is a person, a place, a thing, an idea, or a feeling.

TypeWhat it isExamples
Commona general person, place, or thinglamp, doctor, park
Propera name (capital letter)Maria, Mexico, Los Angeles
Collectivea group of people or animalsclass, band, flock
Abstractan idea or a feeling (you can't touch it)fear, peace, trust
 

Count Nouns

You can count them: one, two, three.

One: a / an / one + noun
Two or more: number + noun + -s
a lamp → two lamps
an idea → three ideas
a doctor → five doctors
Watch out: Never use a or an with a plural. a lamps ✗   lamps ✓
 

Noncount Nouns

We do not count them. They have no plural.

No a / an / one  ·  No -s  ·  Verb: is
some water ✓   a water ✗   two waters ✗
GroupExamples
Liquidswater, coffee, oil
Tiny partsrice, sugar, salt
Groups of thingsmoney, mail, furniture
Ideasadvice, news, luck
School subjectsmath, history, English
Quick check: Can you say "one ___, two ___"? Yes → count. No → noncount.

Exercise 3 — is or are?

One count noun → is  ·  Two or more → are  ·  Noncount → is

Say is or are. Then click to check.

1. The tea ___ very hot.

The tea is very hot. — noncount

2. My shoes ___ under the bed.

My shoes are under the bed. — plural

3. The shampoo ___ in the shower.

The shampoo is in the shower. — noncount

4. My phone ___ in my bag.

My phone is in my bag. — one count noun

5. Three pencils ___ on my desk.

Three pencils are on my desk. — plural

6. I think chemistry ___ hard.

I think chemistry is hard. — school subject, noncount

7. The children ___ at the park.

The children are at the park. — plural (one child, two children)

8. The heat in my apartment ___ off.

The heat in my apartment is off. — noncount

 

Measure Words

How do we count a noncount noun? Use a measure word.

a / an / number + measure word + of + noncount noun
a glass of water
a bowl of soup
a piece of cake
two cans of soda
three boxes of cereal

Exercise 5 — Find the Error

The CAPITALIZED word is wrong. Say the correct word (use the present tense). Then click to check.

1. We need A butter for the cake.

We need some butter for the cake. — butter is noncount (no a). "We need butter for the cake." is also correct.

2. My keys IS in the car.

My keys are in the car. — plural

3. We need three TOMATO for the salad.

We need three tomatoes for the salad. — count, plural

4. The sunshine ARE warm this morning.

The sunshine is warm this morning. — noncount

5. I have A uncle in Miami.

I have an uncle in Miami. — vowel sound ("one uncle" is also correct)

6. He has a new COMPUTERS.

He has a new computer. — a + one thing (no -s)
